I have packed and repacked this bag more times than I can count. I tested this kit in three consecutive typhoon seasons — the year Ulysses put water up to our gate, and the two quieter years after it — and this is the version I have settled on. One bag, by the door, that any adult in the house can grab on the way out.

For under **₱3,000** you can put together a go-bag that actually holds up: clean water, three days of light and charge, a way to hear what is happening, and a blade that will not let you down. Nothing here is showy. Everything here has earned its place by working when the power was off and the rain would not stop.

**Water — Sawyer Squeeze (around ₱1,400).** The single most important thing in the bag. When the mains go cloudy after a storm, this filter turns questionable tap or rainwater into something I will give my children without a second thought. It is small, it never needs batteries, and it keeps working long after a sachet of tablets would have run out.

**Light and charge — Goal Zero Flip 36 (around ₱900).** A power bank that charges a phone and doubles as a torch on the darker nights. Three days of brownout is a long time to be cut off; this keeps one phone alive so we can reach family and read the PAGASA updates.

**Information — Baofeng UV-5R (around ₱600 secondhand).** When the cell towers are congested or down, a radio is how you still hear something. It takes practice and it is not a toy, but it has pulled in local nets during a storm when nothing else worked.

**A blade — Gerber Suspension-NXT (around ₱1,000 when on offer).** A multi-tool, not a survival knife. Pliers, a blade, a driver — the boring things you reach for when a tarpaulin needs cutting or a fitting needs tightening in the dark.

**Warmth — SOL Emergency Bivvy (around ₱700).** Flood water is colder than people expect, and a wet child gets cold fast. This packs to the size of a fist and has kept us warm on one very long night waiting for the water to drop.

## Who this is for

Families in a low-lying barangay, anyone on a high floor who may lose the lift for days, and first-time preppers who want a sensible starting kit rather than a wall of tacticool gear.

## Who this is NOT for

If you already own quality kit, this is a downgrade — buy the better versions individually. And if flooding at your address is life-threatening, no bag replaces leaving early.

## Tested conditions

Packed and lived-with through the 2023, 2024 and 2025 wet seasons. Carried in a flood, soaked through twice, and dried out on the balcony more times than I would like.

## Where to buy

Lazada and Shopee for most items; the Baofeng is often cheaper from local radio hobbyist groups. Buy the Sawyer from a seller with real reviews — the fakes are not worth the risk.
